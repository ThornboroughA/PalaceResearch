# Residences: heated rooms, maru and connecting spaces

**Living research brief, 2026-09-22.** The initial residential configuration uses Gangnyeongjeon and Gyotaejeon as related examples. Their distinctions should remain available as variants while further buildings broaden the reference.

## Inspected evidence

Cho Jaemo's account of Gangnyeongjeon describes a central maru, flanking ondol rooms and surrounding toe spaces. Bunhap doors can separate or join the central hall and peripheral bays; end doors relate to former corridor connections. Residential function also accommodated formal activity. This is the author's account of the historical form and modern reconstruction, not a newly checked measured plan. See [Gangnyeongjeon, notes 001–002](../sources/src-aks-gangnyeongjeon/source.json), 형태 and 위치 및 용도.

Cho Okyeon's Gyotaejeon description uses the approximately 1907 북궐도형 and photographs to discuss the 1888 arrangement. It also has a central maru and flanking heated rooms, but the rear spaces combine maru and ondol, end bays are described as 루, and an annex forms part of its history. Unlike Gangnyeongjeon, it has no front 월대. The present buildings are reconstructions; these descriptions do not establish every current room boundary. See [Gyotaejeon, notes 001–002](../sources/src-aks-gyotaejeon/source.json), 형태 and 변천 및 현황.

The retained [1995 excavation-report images](../sources/src-nrich-residential-excavation-1995/source.json) distinguish excavated remains, a conjectural reconstruction overlay and a reproduction of a historical palace plan. Their visual inspection confirms those different purposes. It does not yet verify room-level construction or the report's reconstruction argument; the full report remains unacquired.

## Provisional fallback proposals

Begin with a complete inhabitable sequence: a timber-floor hall, heated room zones, peripheral circulation and explicit boundaries between them. Every opening should have named spaces on either side and an assumed or documented mode of use. Complete the foundation/platform, frame, floors, partitions, ceiling, roof and access, including a coherent allowance for heating and drainage. Keep those assumptions visible until construction evidence is checked.

Retain selectable differences: projecting front 월대 present or absent, rear bay use, end bays designated 루, annexes and corridor connections. Absence of a 월대 does not imply absence of the building's 기단. The ridge-crest omission described for these two residences is an example-specific candidate, not a rule for all palace dwellings. Do not invent an exact floor-level step, flue layout or door mechanism from the words 마루 and 온돌 alone. A model still receives a sensible complete solution, recorded as a provisional choice.
