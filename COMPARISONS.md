# Cross-building comparisons

These are scoped comparisons of inspected sources, first recorded **2026-09-22**. They inform the [living type briefs](typologies/README.md); they do not establish universal dimensions or approved modeling families.

## C-001: Working halls and residences share functions, not one floor arrangement

Sajeongjeon's official description distinguishes its timber floor without ondol from the heated floors of neighbouring Manchunjeon/Cheonchujeon; the latter's winter use is the source's inference. The authored Gangnyeongjeon account describes a residential combination of maru and ondol which also hosted formal activities. Thus “working” and “residential” help choose a starting configuration, but neither specifies every floor or exclusively determines activity. Sources: [KHS Sajeongjeon, notes 001 and 003](sources/src-khs-sajeongjeon-description/source.json); [AKS Gangnyeongjeon, notes 001–002](sources/src-aks-gangnyeongjeon/source.json).

This comparison was made by the collecting agents from descriptive sources with different historical scopes. Exact level differences, heating assemblies and same-date plans remain unverified; no cross-building dimensions were compared.

## C-002: Residential similarity does not erase variants

Gangnyeongjeon and Gyotaejeon are both described with central maru and flanking heated rooms. Their peripheral spaces and platforms differ: Gangnyeongjeon's account includes a front 월대, whereas Gyotaejeon's explicitly excludes one and describes mixed rear floor types and end 루 bays. Its form discussion draws on the approximately 1907 북궐도형 for the 1888 arrangement; both present buildings are modern reconstructions. Sources: [Gangnyeongjeon, notes 001–002](sources/src-aks-gangnyeongjeon/source.json); [Gyotaejeon, notes 001–002](sources/src-aks-gyotaejeon/source.json).

The resulting residential brief needs variants rather than one cloned footprint. These are two authored accounts within the same reference collection, not independent confirmation of every shared feature. The [1995 excavation images, notes 001–003](sources/src-nrich-residential-excavation-1995/source.json) supply a complementary archaeological route, but their room-level reconstruction argument has not yet been checked.

## C-003: Ranges and connectors are spatially different

Sajeongmun's adjoining ranges contain storage; the wider Sajeongjeon precinct description includes rooms, halls, kitchens and a northern maru accessed from the residential side. Geunjeongjeon's surrounding range also combines open circulation and enclosed uses, with different access sides. Sources: [KHS Sajeongjeon, note-001](sources/src-khs-sajeongjeon-description/source.json); [AKS Sajeongjeon, notes 001–002](sources/src-aks-sajeongjeon/source.json); [AKS Geunjeongjeon, note-001](sources/src-aks-geunjeongjeon/source.json). The precinct descriptions concern adjoining spaces and may share underlying historical plans; they are not three independent surveys.

Separately, the [1395 completion account, note-001](sources/src-sillok-palace-completion-1395/source.json) distinguishes perimeter ranges from passages connecting ranges and halls. This primary textual relationship belongs to the early palace, not automatically to later reconstructions. Together these findings justify separate open-stretch, enclosed-stretch and connector configurations, with mixed use allowed within one range. Actual widths, thresholds and roof junctions remain unmeasured in this pass.
