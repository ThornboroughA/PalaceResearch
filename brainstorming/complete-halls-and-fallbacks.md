# Complete Korean palace buildings: working ideas

**Status: non-canonical brainstorming, begun 2026-09-22.** These are prompts for the owner's separate modeling-workflow discussion. The firm requirement and research priorities are recorded in [PROJECT.md](../PROJECT.md); the proposals below do not approve a component library, numeric defaults or a construction system.

The owner requires a complete, sensibly assembled Korean palace timber building appropriate to the commissioned type. Missing evidence must lead to explicit assumptions and suitable fallback construction. Leaving necessary parts absent is a failed result, even if the surviving modeled parts are highly accurate. Research can remain incomplete while the model is complete at its intended level of detail.

## Two complementary bodies of knowledge

First, extract Sajeongjeon's dimensional attributes into its [building record](../buildings/gbg-sajeongjeon.json). The useful target is a connected account of its plan, heights, supports, floor, openings and roof. The existing bay transcriptions are a start; the next measurements should establish how these parts meet. Preserve measured condition and repair intent separately, and distinguish dimensions still awaiting confirmation from usable architectural measurements.

Second, develop several complete reference configurations with shared vocabulary: halls, residences, covered walkways, and enclosed/mixed ranges. The owner clarified that these need distinct arrangements from the outset. Initial [living research briefs](../typologies/README.md) now collect scoped evidence and provisional proposals; they will be refined as data accumulates. Their purpose is to help assemble whole buildings reliably without flattening meaningful differences.

These two efforts should inform each other. Sajeongjeon can reveal which attributes a complete hall needs and supply documented examples. Each proposed generic relationship should retain the scope of the examples behind it. One building's measurements alone cannot establish a universal standard.

## A possible fallback order

One option for discussion is to prefer building-specific evidence, then a compatible documented comparison, then a researched reference configuration, and finally an explicitly authored modeling choice. The final option matters: an immature reference library must still allow a complete model. The assumed choice remains replaceable when stronger evidence arrives.

Compatibility needs judgment across the assembly. Borrowing a roof profile, column arrangement and opening independently could create a building whose parts do not fit together. A reference configuration could therefore carry connected decisions: supports and spans; floor and threshold; frame and panels; roof supports, eaves and ridge. A changed span may require a corresponding change in the adjoining assembly. The exact rules should be learned through sources and modeling trials.

An economical record might identify the assumption, its source or practical rationale, the affected assembly and the alternative it replaces. Group related assumptions instead of writing an entry for every repeated piece. Actual Sajeongjeon observations remain in research; a chosen substitute and its resulting dimensions belong in the modeling brief.

## Complete first, refine through evidence

A possible authoring sequence is a complete simple building, followed by evidence-driven refinement of proportions, assemblies and visible detail. Every stage should preserve a coherent whole. This could expose missing connections early and give the owner something useful to judge before ornament consumes the effort.

The completion review would ask whether the foundation/platform, supports, floors, enclosure and openings, ceiling and roof form a sensible building at the intended detail level. It would also inspect whether frames fit their host bays, floors meet thresholds and major roof members meet their supports. Necessary geometry should exist regardless of the confidence attached to it. Cutaway presentation can hide selected parts deliberately while preserving the complete underlying building.

## What the present corpus can contribute

The [architectural baseline](../ARCHITECTURAL_BASELINE.md) already separates the support network from room enclosure, relates buildings to their setting and warns against transferring a proposed module outside its evidence. Guo 2005 notes 001–002 provide examples from Geunjeongjeon/Injeongjeon; the Song Chinese cai-fen discussion has its own scope. These help formulate checks, but do not yet supply a complete generic Korean hall or verified default dimensions.

Keep new source-backed findings in source/building records and actual comparisons in [COMPARISONS.md](../COMPARISONS.md). Promote reusable explanations into the baseline when supported. The modeling repository can adopt and version practical reference configurations independently, retaining their assumed parts. A correction should identify affected assumptions and models, allowing deliberate updates without overwriting manual work.

## Questions for the modeling discussion

- Should the first complete model use a simple whole-building pass before close work on one interior assembly?
- What is the minimum convincing building of each type at overview and close interior scales, and which hidden members can use simplified representations?
- How should an authored fallback be chosen and revised when no comparable documented example is available?
- Which changes must propagate together to preserve sensible connections, and which can remain local artistic adjustments?

The immediate research task remains Sajeongjeon's dimensions. The reference configuration can grow in small, revisable steps as dimensional extraction and later modeling reveal actual needs.
